Literature Review

Existing literature on restaurant discovery tools highlights a growing emphasis on personalization, yet many platforms still rely heavily on algorithmic popularity rankings, aggregated reviews, or broad categorical filters. Blogs and industry analyses frequently discuss the importance of understanding user personas, such as “the foodie,” “the trendsetter,” or “the comfort‑seeker”, as a way to tailor recommendations more effectively. These sources emphasize that dining decisions are shaped not only by cuisine preferences but also by emotional, social, and experiential factors. However, most mainstream tools fail to integrate these nuanced taste profiles into their recommendation logic. Research on taste profiling and persona‑based design suggests that users respond positively to systems that reflect their identity and values. Studies in UX and personalization argue that persona‑driven frameworks can reduce decision fatigue and increase user satisfaction by narrowing choices to those that align with a user’s self‑concept. Meanwhile, food blogs and city‑specific dining guides offer rich qualitative insights into how different types of diners engage with restaurant culture across major metropolitan areas. Yet these sources rarely intersect with geospatial visualization or interactive mapping. This gap, between persona‑based dining insights and spatial exploration tools, motivated our project. While the literature acknowledges the importance of taste profiles and the value of personalization, few tools combine these concepts with interactive mapping. Our project contributes to this space by merging persona‑driven curation with geospatial visualization, demonstrating how taste profiles can be operationalized into a more intuitive and user‑centered discovery experience.
