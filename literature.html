Literature Review

In exploring existing restaurant discovery tools, it was obvious that while personalization is often emphasized, most platforms still rely heavily on popularity rankings, aggregated reviews, or broad filters that don’t fully capture how people actually choose where to eat. Seeing restaurant seekers through the lens of individual profiles like “trend-chasers” and “comfort-seekers” highlights that dining decisions go beyond just cuisine and are shaped by mood, social context, and overall experience. Research on taste profiling and persona-based design shows that people are more engaged when recommendations reflect their identity and preferences. UX studies also suggest that this kind of personalization can reduce decision fatigue and make the process feel more intuitive. At the same time, food blogs and city-specific guides offer detailed, experience-driven insights into how different types of diners interact with restaurant culture, but these insights rarely connect to create an intuitive platform. This gap between persona-driven insights and spatial discovery is what inspired this site. It bridges the disconnect by combining taste-based personas with geospatial visualization, creating a system that feels more aligned with how people actually think about dining.
