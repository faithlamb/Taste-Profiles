Conclusion

This project demonstrates that personalized taste profiles can significantly enhance the user experience of restaurant discovery tools by aligning recommendations with individual dining identities. Through curated personas and interactive geospatial visualization, we showed how users can explore restaurants in a way that feels intuitive, relevant, and reflective of their preferences. The maps make abstract taste profiles tangible, revealing spatial patterns that deepen our understanding of how different types of diners engage with urban food culture. Our findings suggest that integrating taste profiles into restaurant discovery platforms could reduce decision fatigue, increase user satisfaction, and create more meaningful connections between diners and their environments. At the same time, the project raises new questions: How might these profiles evolve with user behavior over time? Could machine learning personalize maps dynamically? What additional data—such as price, ambiance, or dietary needs—could further refine the experience? By bridging persona‑based design with interactive mapping, our project contributes to a growing conversation about personalization in digital tools. It highlights the potential for restaurant discovery systems to become more human‑centered, expressive, and engaging through the thoughtful use of taste profiles.
